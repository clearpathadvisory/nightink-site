Why it sounds different

Every competitor is playing you a recording

A rain track is a few seconds of audio repeating for eight hours. Once you have heard the seam, you cannot unhear it — and at 3am you have nothing to do but listen for it.

Duskloom has no audio files at all. Rain is noise through filters with individual drops landing at random. Ocean waves swell every eight to fifteen seconds and never twice the same. A fireplace is a bed of embers with crackles scheduled as they happen.

Because it generates rather than streams, it works in airplane mode, in a basement, on a flight. It makes no network requests at all — there is nothing to collect and no account to make.

Nothing loops

Two nights of the same mix are never the same audio. There is no seam because there is no recording.

Questions

Reasonable things to ask

Does it really not loop?

Really. There are no audio files in the app to loop. Noise is generated sample by sample, and events like raindrops, thunder and crackles are scheduled at random times as they occur. You can leave the same mix running for eight hours and no two minutes will be identical.

Will it keep playing when my phone locks?

Yes. It declares itself an audio app to iOS and runs a playback service on Android, so it plays all night with the screen off and shows on your lock screen.

Do you collect anything?

No, and not as a policy choice — the app makes no network requests, so there is no channel through which anything could leave your phone. Put it in airplane mode and it behaves identically. Your levels and saved mixes are stored on the device.

What is free, exactly?

All seven noise colours, plus rain, ocean, wind, fireplace, box fan, stream and crickets. Unlimited playback, background audio, the sleep timer and the paced breathing guide are never locked. Inside the app, tap any paid sound and it plays for thirty seconds, so you hear exactly what you would be buying before you pay.

Is there a subscription?

No, and there will not be one. A subscription pays for recording, licensing and hosting. We have none of those costs.

Does it help with tinnitus?

Many people use coloured noise for it, and Duskloom generates white, pink, brown, green, blue, grey and violet noise properly rather than playing a recording of them. We make no medical claim — talk to an audiologist about what is right for you.
